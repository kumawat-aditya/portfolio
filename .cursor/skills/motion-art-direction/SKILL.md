# Motion Art Direction

Animation is part of the visual language, not decoration added after layout.

## Fundamental rule

Movement must communicate something.

A movement may represent:

- curiosity
- discovery
- resistance
- physicality
- transformation
- cause and effect
- technical behavior
- humor
- transition

## Avoid repetitive AI animation

Do not automatically use:

- fade in
- slide upward
- scale from 0.95 to 1

for every section.

Repeated animation patterns make the website feel generated.

## Motion vocabulary

Elements may:

- drift
- orbit
- stretch
- rotate
- detach
- collide
- resist
- pin
- follow
- accelerate
- slow down
- disappear
- reappear
- transform
- split apart
- recombine

Use different motion behaviors according to context.

## Scroll

Scrolling can be treated as a storytelling mechanism.

Use scroll to manipulate:

- spatial relationships
- scale
- depth
- camera movement
- diagrams
- typography
- objects
- scene composition

Prefer meaningful scroll-linked choreography over simple reveal animations.

## Physical behavior

Where appropriate, elements should feel like they have:

- weight
- inertia
- friction
- momentum
- spatial relationships

Do not fake physicality everywhere.

Use it selectively.

## Micro-interactions

Use subtle reactions to user behavior.

Examples:

- cursor proximity
- hover
- dragging
- scrolling
- entering a scene
- leaving a scene

Interactions should reward curiosity.

## Silence

Some sections should have almost no animation.

Stillness is part of the motion language.

## Performance

Prefer compositor-friendly properties.

Avoid unnecessary layout-triggering animation.

Large animation systems must degrade gracefully.

Respect reduced-motion preferences.
